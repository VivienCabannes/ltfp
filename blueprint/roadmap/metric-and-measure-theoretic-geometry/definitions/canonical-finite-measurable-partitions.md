# Canonical finite measurable partitions

Let `alpha` be a countably generated measurable space. Mathlib defines a sequence

`MeasurableSpace.countablePartition alpha n : Set (Set alpha)`.

It starts from `{univ}` and repeatedly splits every current cell by the next set in a chosen enumeration of a chosen countable generating family. Thus the construction is a definite Mathlib object, but "canonical" here does not mean independent of those choices.

For every natural number `n`, this is a finite family of measurable subsets of `alpha`. Distinct members are disjoint and the union of all members is the universal set. Empty cells are retained, so these are partitions in the finite, pairwise-disjoint, covering sense rather than families of necessarily nonempty blocks. The measurable spaces generated by successive families are increasing; this does not assert that the families themselves are nested. The sigma-algebra generated by the union of all levels is the original measurable space.

No measure, finiteness-of-measure condition, probability-measure assumption, nonempty-type assumption, or separation condition is involved. Countable generation of the measurable space is the only structural hypothesis beyond the measurable space itself.
